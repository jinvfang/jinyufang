In Class Plotter Exercise

Electromechanical DesignerElectromechanical Design, Boston UniversitySep 2026

Project overview

Two Lead-Screw Stages That Draw From G-Code.

In class, I built a single linear stage, then stacked two of them into an X–Y plotter that draws shapes with a marker from G-code.

The exercise was a first step toward the larger 2.5-degree-of-freedom project: get one axis moving accurately, then coordinate two. The same motors and control board went on to drive my 2.5 DoF Carnival Fishing machine.

Key Work & Outcomes

  • Built a lead-screw linear stage driven by a NEMA 17 stepper.
  • Stacked two stages at right angles to make an X–Y plotter.
  • Drove both axes from an MKS Base board using G-code.
  • Drew a square and a circle with a marker as motion tests.

Linear stage

One Axis First

A stepper turns a lead screw, and a brass nut converts that rotation into straight-line travel.

The NEMA 17 motor connects to the lead screw through a flexible coupler, which absorbs small misalignments. The brass nut is fixed to a foam-board carriage, and a pillow-block bearing supports the far end of the screw.

Plotting

Two Axes, One Marker

With one stage stacked on the other and a marker in a printed holder, the plotter drew shapes from G-code.
