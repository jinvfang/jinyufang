12-Tungstosilicic Acid Synthesis

Capstone ResearcherJan 2025 — May 2025

Project overview

Synthesizing a solid acid catalyst and testing whether it works.

A chemistry capstone synthesizing the Keggin-type polyoxometalate 12-tungstosilicic acid and testing it as a catalyst for dehydrating cyclohexanol to cyclohexene.

Working in a team of three, we synthesized 12-tungstosilicic acid from sodium tungstate dihydrate and sodium silicate, adding concentrated HCl dropwise near boiling, then isolated the acid by diethyl ether extraction in a separatory funnel and crystallized it on a water bath. The product was characterized by UV-Vis spectroscopy (Cary 60) and IR spectroscopy and compared against literature spectra.

We then used the acid to catalyze the dehydration of cyclohexanol, distilling off the cyclohexene–water azeotrope, and analyzed the distillate by 1H NMR in CDCl3 against lab-grade cyclohexene. The acid itself matched the literature, but the NMR showed little cyclohexene and strong side-product peaks, which we traced to contamination. We proposed fixes including a high-boiling chaser solvent such as toluene and salting out bulk water with sodium chloride.

Key work & outcomes

  • Confirmed successful synthesis: UV-Vis λmax of 245–265 nm, within the expected 200–260 nm band.
  • Matched literature IR peaks: a broad band near 3369 cm−1, 1611 cm−1, and sharp peaks from 727–1015 cm−1.
  • Used 1H NMR to show the catalytic run produced mostly side products rather than cyclohexene (weak 5.67 ppm signal).
  • Proposed purification and reaction-condition improvements for future catalytic testing.
