Composting & Carbon Emissions Analysis

Sustainability Analyst InternSustainable House, SydneyJan 2026 — Jun 2026

Project overview

Turning climate reports into graphs people can read.

I turned City of Sydney emissions, temperature, and water data into clear graphs for a meeting with the Lord Mayor, and did hands-on community composting in Chippendale.

I interned with Michael Mobbs's Sustainable House, an urban sustainability practice run from a Chippendale home that has been off the grid for water, sewage, and energy since 1996. I worked in a team of four to five interns, splitting my time between research and data visualization and field work on the neighbourhood's Coolseats, composting benches that people can sit on.

I began by helping another intern check environmental data and draft graphs. When that intern left in February, I took over the whole graphs document. I researched the Council's carbon reporting in the City of Sydney Green Reports (2015–2025), its Public Disclosure Statements, Bureau of Meteorology temperature records, and the 2025 National Climate Risk Assessment. Some data could not be found, such as emissions from waste contractors, so I narrowed the analysis to the Council's published figures, which are complete and verifiable. I then took the document from Revision K to Revision V through feedback from Michael, the team, and a Monash University research fellow.

The final document shows that the Council cut its operational emissions by 78% from the 2006 baseline, missing its 2025 target of 80%. Reaching the 2030 target (85%) needs another 7 points, and net zero by 2035 needs another 22. In 2023/24, 72.5% of the carbon offsets the Council bought came from overseas projects. The document argues that the targets can only be met with community partners such as Chippendale, whose offer to trial climate solutions Councillors accepted unanimously in June 2025.

Key work & outcomes

  • Led the City of Sydney graphs document from Revision K to V, simplifying dense reports into single-message visuals such as a thermometer for temperature rise and stacked bars for Council emissions by source.
  • Built the Excel analysis of Council emissions (buildings, street lighting, parks, fleet, other) from 2006 to 2025 against the 2025, 2030, and 2035 targets.
  • Charted reported carbon offsets (about 483,000 tCO2-e) and their split between Australian and international projects.
  • Maintained Coolseats across Chippendale, including outside Café Giulia, and helped set up new Coolseats at a public school. This involved aerating compost, pruning, planting, and bagging more than 10 bags of compost for the community.
  • Co-wrote the Coolseats “Worms at Work” live compost data blog, graphing the Melbourne trial's pH, ambient temperature, and compost moisture and explaining how to read them.
  • Researched soil bulk density and urban heat island mitigation to support Sustainable House's urban cooling work.

Field work

Composting in Chippendale

Coolseats turn street food scraps into compost that goes back to the neighbourhood.

I learned to use an auger, secateurs, compost bins, and a harvesting knife, and worked out a rotating aeration schedule with the other interns to keep the benches active. Moving compost also gave me a practical feel for torque and resistance, which connected to my Engineering Mechanics coursework.
